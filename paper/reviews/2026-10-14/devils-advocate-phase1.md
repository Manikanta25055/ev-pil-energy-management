## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory): I will ask whether the comparison between learned and rule-based energy management was built fairly enough that a negative result for the learned side can be believed. That means equal tuning effort and budget for both arms, run counts with intervals, a held-out split that was not used to choose the controller, and enough detail (seeds, build switches, firmware, board configuration) for a stranger to reproduce it. My adversarial stance is that a result of "learned is not better" is cheap to produce by under-tuning the learned arm, and that a pre-registered gate only protects the authors if the gate itself was set neutrally.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory): I will test whether the claims match what the field actually knows about learned control, rule-based and optimisation-based energy management, state estimation and embedded inference. I will check that the baselines are the ones practitioners would call strong (not a straw rule set), that drive-cycle, powertrain and plant terminology is used correctly, that prior work is represented fairly in both directions, and that MEASURED on-target results are kept apart from SIMULATED host-only ones.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory): This is my main dimension. I will trace the chain from evidence to the central thesis and look for places where it breaks. In particular I will test whether a negative result on one algorithm, one tuning budget, one vehicle model and one board is stated as a general claim about learned controllers. I will also test whether the authors' own discovered plant artefact, which once produced a reversed conclusion, undermines trust in every other result, and whether the paper treats that as a closed incident or as evidence about its own measurement process. I will look for post-hoc reframing, confirmation bias, unsupported mechanism claims, and an unanswered "so what?".

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high): I will check whether a reader from machine learning, power electronics or embedded systems can follow the framing without insider knowledge, and whether claims that reach across fields (for example that the finding holds for learning-based control in general, or that on-device cost transfers to other microcontrollers) are actually substantiated rather than asserted.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal): I will check that the manuscript is organised so that the claim, its scope and its limits are easy to find, that tables and figures carry run counts, units and intervals, that the pre-registration story is stated in one place and consistently, and that the layout fits IEEE Access conventions. I will not treat mock author names, affiliation or dates as defects, as the contract directs.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
dimension_id: D1
what_to_look_for: (a) A tuning budget for the learned controller stated in comparable units (compute hours, trials, reward-shaping iterations) and matched against the effort spent on the rules; whether the rule set was hand-tuned on the same cycles used for evaluation. (b) Run counts, seeds and confidence intervals or paired tests on every headline percentage; whether a "1 of 16" style count of wins has a stated null and multiple-comparison handling. (c) Train, validation and test separation by drive cycle, and whether gate thresholds, the actor, and the rules were fixed before the test cells were run. (d) Evidence that the pre-registration is real: dated, immutable, and with deviations listed. (e) Reproducibility: firmware build switches, header-presence compile-time arm selection, board and USB configuration, frame-drop handling, and whether code and logs are available. (f) Hardware-measurement hygiene, including excluded or re-run cells and the criterion for excluding them. (g) Energy comparison on a common scale, with shed cabin heat or other uncharged loads accounted for.
what_triggers_block: Any of: the learned arm received a visibly smaller or undocumented tuning budget than the rules and the paper still draws a general conclusion; the headline comparison has no run counts or intervals; cells were dropped or re-run without a pre-stated rule and the dropped cells favour one side; thresholds or the actor were selected on the test set; the energy metric charges the arms unequally so that the ranking could flip under a fair scale; the pre-registration cannot be verified or was amended after seeing results without disclosure.
what_triggers_warn: Run counts or intervals present for some but not all claims; a tuning budget is described qualitatively but not quantified; single-seed training of the learned arm; exclusion rules exist but were set after the first campaign; reproducibility artefacts promised but not specified (no seeds, no build flags, no logs); a small number of board cells with high variance relative to the reported effect size (for example sub-1 percent differences with no interval).

### D2: domain_accuracy
dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: (a) Whether the rule-based baseline is a recognised strong baseline (for example thresholded or equivalent-consumption rules with tuned parameters) or a deliberately simple one. (b) Whether the learned controller is a representative of current practice (policy class, training algorithm, observation design) or an outdated or weak variant, and whether comparison to optimal or dynamic-programming bounds is given. (c) Correct use of drive-cycle, plant-model, regeneration, power-ceiling and energy-accounting terminology. (d) Fair citation of prior work that reports learned energy management winning, with an explanation of why the paper's result differs. (e) A clean line between MEASURED on-target results and SIMULATED or host-only results throughout, including in the abstract and conclusions. (f) Whether the Kalman-filter and fault-detector claims are described in terms the state-estimation literature would accept.
what_triggers_block: A factual error in a core domain claim (for example a mis-stated energy metric, a plant model that violates basic physics in a way that determines the ranking, or a baseline presented as state of the art that is not); a SIMULATED result reported as MEASURED on the target board in the abstract or conclusions; prior work on learned energy management misrepresented so that the negative result looks like consensus.
what_triggers_warn: Baseline strength asserted but not benchmarked against an optimality bound; prior positive results acknowledged only briefly or only in the introduction; one learned algorithm family tested while text speaks of learning in general; MEASURED versus SIMULATED labelling present in tables but inconsistent in prose; terminology used loosely (for example "accuracy", "gain" or "cost" without units or definition).

### D3: argumentative_coherence
dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: (a) The exact scope of the central claim versus the scope of the evidence: one algorithm, one tuning budget, one vehicle model, one board, one set of drive cycles. Any sentence whose subject is "learned controllers" or "learning" rather than "this controller as tuned". (b) The plant artefact the authors discovered, where a conclusion reversed after the plant changed: whether the paper audits which other results depended on the same plant, shows they were re-derived, and states what class of artefact could still be undetected. (c) Whether the comparison design could favour the rules (rules tuned on the evaluation cycles, gates set after seeing early results, metric chosen after the first campaign) and whether the pre-registered gates were met, missed or quietly redefined. (d) Mechanism claims: each stated cause must be backed by a logged driving quantity, not by narrative. (e) Selective reporting: the number of arms, cycles and cells tried versus shown; whether sub-results that favour the learned side (for example the estimation result) are used to hedge the negative headline. (f) Alternative explanations: insufficient training, observation-space limits, reward mis-specification, quantisation on the microcontroller, or a compressed model losing the advantage. (g) The "so what?": if the negative result is right, what should a practitioner change, and is that conclusion different from what a rule-based engineer already assumes.
what_triggers_block: Any of: the title, abstract or conclusion states or implies a general negative result for learned energy management while the evidence covers one algorithm and one tuning budget; the authors' own artefact discovery invalidates a result on which a headline claim still rests and the paper does not re-derive it; the stated thesis contradicts the paper's own table (for example claims parity or superiority that the reported interval excludes); the pre-registered acceptance gates were redefined after results and the paper presents the outcome as a clean pass or fail; a simpler alternative explanation (under-tuning, or the plant artefact) fits the data at least as well as the stated mechanism and is not addressed.
what_triggers_warn: Scope limits are present but confined to a limitations paragraph while headline sentences stay general; the artefact is disclosed but its implications for other results are argued rather than re-tested; one or more mechanism claims lack a logged quantity; the contribution is mostly "a careful null result" without stating what decision it changes; positive side results are used rhetorically to soften the main finding; alternative explanations are named but not tested.

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: (a) Whether a machine-learning reader can see what was trained, on what, with what budget, and whether an embedded reader can see the on-device cost (memory, latency, energy per inference) reported next to every accuracy-like number. (b) Whether a power-systems or vehicle-dynamics reader can see how the plant, ceiling and energy accounting are defined. (c) Claims that cross fields: that findings transfer to other microcontrollers, vehicles or learning methods; whether each has support. (d) Whether the pre-registration and acceptance-gate method, borrowed from clinical or social-science practice, is explained and justified for engineering, and whether its value as a method is demonstrated or only asserted. (e) Definitions of jargon (processor-in-the-loop, gate, arm, actor) at first use.
what_triggers_block: A cross-field implication that is central to the paper's stated contribution (for example that pre-registered gates are a validated method for embedded ML evaluation, or that the result transfers across hardware classes) is asserted with no supporting evidence, and removing it would leave the contribution unclear.
what_triggers_warn: On-device cost missing for some reported results; transfer to other boards or vehicles implied but untested; method framing (pre-registration) not justified for readers outside the authors' sub-field; key terms undefined or used inconsistently; implications for adjacent fields stated in the conclusion that the body does not develop.

### D5: writing_and_structure
dimension_id: D5
what_to_look_for: (a) Whether a reader can find, within the abstract and introduction, the exact claim, its scope and the main limitation. (b) Consistency of numbers between abstract, tables, figures and conclusion, and between repeated mentions of the same gate or campaign. (c) Tables and figures that carry units, run counts, intervals and the MEASURED or SIMULATED label; readable axes at print size. (d) Logical section order (problem, design, gates, results, artefact audit, discussion) and signposting of what was fixed in advance versus decided later. (e) Length proportionate to content at about 11,900 words and 18 pages; repeated material; promotional or inflated wording. (f) Venue conventions for IEEE Access (reference format, figure quality, structured abstract length). Author names, affiliation and dates are placeholders and are not counted.
what_triggers_block: Numeric contradictions between abstract and results on a headline quantity, or missing figures or tables that make the central claim unverifiable from the manuscript.
what_triggers_warn: Several tables or figures without run counts, units or intervals; the pre-registration timeline told in several places with small inconsistencies; inflated or evaluative wording without a measurement behind it; heavy repetition between sections; figure text too small or labels ambiguous; reference-format deviations.
