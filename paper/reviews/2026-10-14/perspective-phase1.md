## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory): From my embedded-ML and reproducibility angle, this asks whether a reader could rebuild the study and trust its numbers. For a processor-in-the-loop study, that means the plant model, controller code, build switches, random seeds, run counts and uncertainty intervals are all stated, and the pre-registered gates are fixed before the data. I also read it as asking whether the simulator itself was audited for exploitable shortcuts, since a learned controller can win by exploiting the plant rather than by being better.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory): Claims about EV energy management, learned controllers, state estimation and fault detection must match current evidence, and prior work must be represented fairly. From my side, this also covers correct use of safety and microcontroller vocabulary (fault detection, diagnostic coverage, latency, memory footprint, deployment cost) and correct treatment of sim-to-real and reward-hacking literature. The MEASURED versus SIMULATED split in the contract hints is a domain-accuracy matter as well.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory): The central thesis, which I expect to be a comparison of learned and rule-based control under pre-registered gates, must follow from the evidence. Negative or reversed results must be reported as such, and conclusions must not outrun what was measured. A story that changes during the study, such as an earlier positive result later traced to an artefact, must be told consistently.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high): This is my home dimension. Can a reader from functional safety, general ML, or control outside EV energy management follow the framing, and do the interdisciplinary claims hold? I will check that terms are defined, that lessons on simulator artefacts and negative results are stated for the wider ML community, and that any safety-relevant statement about fault detection is supported rather than implied.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal): Organisation, clarity, figure and table quality, and IEEE Access conventions. I judge it as a non-specialist: acronyms defined on first use, tables readable, figures legible with units, and the path from question to gates to results easy to follow.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
dimension_id: D1
what_to_look_for: Pre-registered gates stated with thresholds and a record of when they were fixed; run counts per cell with intervals or other spread; seeds and repeat policy; target hardware, toolchain and build configuration named; how the controller arm is selected and how that was checked; plant validation and any audit for exploitable simulator behaviour; dropped-frame or timing-contamination handling on the board; a parity or equivalence check between host and firmware implementations; a code and data availability statement; on-device cost (latency, flash, RAM) reported alongside any accuracy or energy figure.
what_triggers_block: Headline comparison rests on single runs or unreported run counts with no intervals; or gates were changed after seeing results without disclosure; or the learned-versus-rule comparison is run on different plants, cycles or configurations without saying so; or the paper cannot show which controller was actually running on the board; or accuracy or energy numbers carry no deployment cost and no way to reproduce them.
what_triggers_warn: Intervals present for some results but not all; seeds or build settings described only partly; plant artefact checks described but not shown; board contamination or dropped frames mentioned without a stated exclusion rule; no artefact or code availability statement; hardware-measured and host-simulated results sharing one table without clear labels.

### D2: domain_accuracy
dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Correct description of rule-based and learned energy management (including RL baselines), Kalman and learned state estimation, and fault detection; fair comparison with classical optimal control baselines; correct use of terms such as processor-in-the-loop, hardware-in-the-loop and software-in-the-loop; a clear split between MEASURED (on target) and SIMULATED (host-only) claims; energy compared on a fair basis (for example, with auxiliary loads charged consistently); accurate microcontroller resource figures; citation of sim-to-real, specification gaming and benchmark-artefact literature from outside EV work.
what_triggers_block: A headline domain claim is factually wrong or contradicted by the paper's own data; simulated results are presented as measured on hardware; the rule baseline is a strawman that no practitioner would deploy, so the negative or positive verdict is meaningless; energy ranking depends on an unfair accounting that the paper does not disclose.
what_triggers_warn: Terminology used loosely (PIL, HIL and SIL mixed); baseline strength argued but not shown; key related work in learned energy management, TinyML deployment or reward hacking is missing; a fault-detection or estimator result described in wording stronger than the measurement supports; resource figures given without the compiler settings or board used.

### D3: argumentative_coherence
dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Whether the abstract, results, discussion and conclusion state the same verdict; whether the abstract and the registered gates agree on what counts as success; whether the narrative handles a reversed or corrected earlier result honestly and consistently; whether each claimed finding has the evidence it needs in the main text; whether the explanation offered for an effect (for example a plant artefact) was tested by measuring the driving quantity rather than inferred; whether limitations and untested scope are stated.
what_triggers_block: The conclusion claims a win or a usable result that the reported gates and numbers do not support; the paper contradicts itself on the main verdict between sections; a mechanism is asserted as established with no measurement of it and the conclusions depend on it.
what_triggers_warn: Some claims are worded more strongly than the effect size or interval allows; a post-hoc explanation is given without a test; secondary claims (fault detection, alternative architectures) are summarised more favourably than their tables; limitations section is thin or generic; the contribution is framed as novelty when the data support a negative or confirmatory result, with no clear statement of why that matters.

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether an ML reader can take away the lesson on simulator artefacts, specification gaming and pre-registration as method; whether an automotive-safety reader finds an honest statement of what the fault-detection results do and do not show (no implied ISO 26262 or SOTIF compliance, no ASIL claim, clear scope as an advisory or monitoring function); whether a firmware reader can judge feasibility from reported flash, RAM, cycle counts and the target microcontroller class, and whether those figures transfer to automotive-grade parts; whether jargon from EV energy management is defined; whether the paper discusses what happens when a learned component fails or is out of distribution, including a fallback to the rule controller.
what_triggers_block: The paper makes safety-relevant claims (the detector is dependable, safe to deploy, or suitable for a safety function) without any evidence, failure-mode analysis, or stated scope; or the generalisation claim to other platforms or vehicles is made with no support while the study uses a single hobby-class board and a single simulated plant.
what_triggers_warn: Safety vocabulary used without definition or without a statement that no safety case is claimed; board results presented as indicative of automotive-grade deployment with no discussion of the gap; lessons for the wider ML community left implicit; key project-specific terms (for example, what a gate is) not defined for a reader outside the subfield; fault-detector limits (for example, failure on real logged data) not tied to what that means for use.

### D5: writing_and_structure
dimension_id: D5
what_to_look_for: IEEE Access section order and abstract length; consistent notation; acronyms expanded on first use; a table or figure for the gates and their outcomes that a reader can scan; labelled axes, units and legible text size in figures; captions that stand alone; separation of MEASURED and SIMULATED results in tables; length proportionate to content at about 11,900 words; reference style and completeness; placeholder author details are not a defect per the contract.
what_triggers_block: The manuscript cannot be followed because sections are missing or out of order, results tables are unreadable or unlabelled, or text is so unclear that the main result cannot be identified.
what_triggers_warn: Dense results sections with many unlabelled abbreviations; figures with small text or missing units; the same result repeated across sections; tables mixing measured and simulated rows without a marker; inconsistent terminology for the same quantity; heavy use of internal project labels (codes for experiments) that an outside reader cannot decode.
