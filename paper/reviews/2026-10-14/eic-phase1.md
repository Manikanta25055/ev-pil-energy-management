## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory): From the Associate Editor's seat for vehicular technology, this dimension is a test of whether the study design can support its stated comparison of a learned energy-management controller against a rule-based one on a microcontroller. The design must be fair to both arms, the data and drive cycles must be handled without leakage, and the statistics must carry run counts and intervals. A reader should be able to reproduce the processor-in-the-loop (PIL) setup, which means the toolchain, firmware build, seeds, and the pre-registered gates are all stated well enough to repeat. For IEEE Access, soundness and reproducibility count for more than novelty.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory): The manuscript must describe EV powertrain control, energy accounting, PIL/HIL practice, reinforcement learning and state estimation correctly, and must represent prior work fairly. Energy and efficiency figures must be computed on a defensible basis, and plant-model limitations that could bias a comparison must be disclosed. Terminology such as HIL versus PIL, rule-based versus learned, and measured versus simulated must be used consistently and correctly.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory): The thesis, the pre-registered gates, the results and the conclusions must agree with one another. If a gate fails or a result reverses, the text should say so and follow the evidence rather than argue around it. The title, abstract, introduction and conclusion must make the same claim, and the contribution must not exceed what the evidence shows.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high): Readers from adjacent fields (embedded ML, control, RL, automotive software) should be able to follow the framing and see why the result matters to them. Claims that reach into other areas, such as pre-registration practice borrowed from empirical science or deployment cost on a microcontroller, should be backed by evidence and defined for non-specialists.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal): The manuscript should be organised along IEEE Access conventions, with clear exposition, figures and tables that are legible and consistent with the text, and a length proportionate to the content. This dimension matters least to the decision but affects reader trust.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
dimension_id: D1
what_to_look_for: (1) Evaluation on target hardware, with every claim labelled MEASURED (on the microcontroller in the loop) or SIMULATED (host only), and no host-only number presented as a board result. (2) Run counts, number of seeds, drive cycles and repeats stated, with confidence intervals or equivalent spread for every headline comparison. (3) Pre-registered gates written down with numeric thresholds, with evidence they were fixed before the results and an account of any later change. (4) Train/validation/test separation of drive cycles, and no tuning on the test cycles. (5) A like-for-like comparison: the same plant, same cycles, same energy metric, and the rule baseline tuned with effort comparable to the learned controller. (6) Energy compared on a common basis, for example with shed cabin heat or auxiliary load charged back, and not raw Wh/km alone. (7) Reported dropped frames, timing and on-device cost (flash, RAM, latency) next to accuracy or energy figures. (8) Build and run reproducibility: controller arm selection, compile flags, seeds, firmware version, code and data availability, and parity tests between host and firmware implementations of any controller.
what_triggers_block: Any of these: a headline comparison with no run counts or intervals; measured and simulated results mixed so that a reader cannot tell which supports the main claim; a learned-versus-rule verdict that rests on a plant artefact (for example a power ceiling that caps billed energy but not motion) that the paper does not identify; test cycles used for tuning or gate selection; gates changed after the results without disclosure; a build or arm-selection ambiguity that could mean the baseline was run as the ablation or the reverse; no information from which the experiments could be repeated.
what_triggers_warn: Run counts or intervals given for some comparisons but not all; gates stated but the date or lock-in evidence is thin; the rule baseline's tuning effort is undocumented; on-device cost is reported for some models but not others; boards showing dropped frames or timing contamination without a stated exclusion rule; the energy basis is stated but not shown to be stable under a different cabin-heat or auxiliary-load convention; code or data availability promised but not specific; small numbers of board repeats with wide intervals read as firm conclusions.

### D2: domain_accuracy
dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: (1) Correct use of PIL versus HIL: the plant is simulated on a host while the controller runs on the target, so the paper should not call it hardware-in-the-loop for the powertrain. (2) A plant model with a documented battery, motor, drivetrain, thermal and cabin treatment, and a statement of what it cannot represent. (3) Energy accounting that is physically consistent, including regeneration limits, power ceilings and auxiliary loads. (4) Fair coverage of prior work on rule-based, optimisation-based (DP, ECMS, MPC) and RL energy management, and on embedded or quantised inference; the baselines named are the strong ones, not weak straw men. (5) State-estimation claims (for example Kalman filtering versus learned estimators) matched to known behaviour under noise. (6) Correct microcontroller facts: clock, memory, numeric formats, timing budgets. (7) Standard drive-cycle names and properties used correctly.
what_triggers_block: A factual error that changes a conclusion: for example a claimed energy saving that comes from a model inconsistency such as motion not being limited by a stated power constraint; calling a host-side simulation a hardware result; a misdescribed baseline that makes the comparison unfair; claims contradicted by the cited literature; energy figures that are not physically plausible for the cycle and vehicle class and are not explained.
what_triggers_warn: Imprecise or inconsistent terminology (HIL/PIL, deployed/simulated, accuracy/energy); related work that omits obvious strong baselines such as DP, ECMS or MPC; vehicle or battery parameters given without source; plant limitations listed only in passing; claims about generality to real vehicles stated without qualification; citations to secondary sources for primary results.

### D3: argumentative_coherence
dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: (1) A single, falsifiable thesis stated in the abstract, matched in the introduction, results and conclusion. (2) A clear chain from the pre-registered gates to the gate outcomes to the verdict, including gates that failed. (3) Honest treatment of negative or reversed results: if an earlier positive finding was later traced to an artefact, the paper says so and states what survives. (4) Conclusions that stay within the measured evidence: no claim of a learned advantage that the numbers do not show, and no claim of a rule advantage that the intervals do not support. (5) Consistency of numbers between abstract, tables, text and conclusion. (6) Contribution statements that match what was delivered, with no promise of generality beyond the tested cycles, vehicle, and board. (7) Absence of fallacies such as cherry-picked cycles, post-hoc gate redefinition, or treating a non-significant difference as proof of equivalence.
what_triggers_block: The headline claim contradicts the paper's own tables or gate outcomes; gate failure is recast as success without evidence; a conclusion depends on a result the paper itself identifies as an artefact; numbers for the same quantity differ between sections in a way that changes the verdict; the title promises a comparison that the evidence cannot decide and the paper does not say so.
what_triggers_warn: Abstract or conclusion worded more strongly than the results section; a favourable subset of cycles or metrics highlighted while the full gate table is relegated to an appendix; absence of a difference described as equivalence without a margin; an unexplained shift in framing between introduction and conclusion; minor numerical inconsistencies that do not change the verdict.

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: (1) Definitions of EV-specific terms (energy management, PIL, regeneration, cabin heat) and of ML terms (policy, reward, quantisation) sufficient for a reader from the other field. (2) A stated link between the embedded-deployment results and the wider question of whether learned controllers justify their cost on small processors. (3) Whether the pre-registration approach is explained and its value to control and embedded ML communities argued, not only asserted. (4) Practical implications for automotive software teams, for example verification effort, timing certification, and maintainability, supported by the data reported. (5) Interdisciplinary claims (for example about reproducibility practice) tied to what the study actually did.
what_triggers_block: Core claims depend on terminology or concepts left undefined so that an adjacent-field reader cannot judge them; broad claims about the field (for example that learned control is or is not suited to automotive microcontrollers in general) are made from one vehicle, one board, and a few cycles with no qualification.
what_triggers_warn: Jargon used without definition in the abstract or introduction; implications for industry stated without support from cost, timing or safety data; the pre-registration discussion is brief and does not say what it changed in practice; limited positioning against work in adjacent communities such as embedded ML benchmarking.

### D5: writing_and_structure
dimension_id: D5
what_to_look_for: (1) IEEE Access structure and length: abstract, keywords, introduction with contributions, related work, method, results, discussion, limitations, conclusion, references. (2) Figures and tables readable at print size, with captions that state what is measured, the number of runs, and the units; text inside figures large enough to read. (3) Consistent notation and symbols across sections. (4) Prose that is plain and direct, with few inflated or evaluative words that have no measurement behind them. (5) A limitations section that names the plant, board, cycle and sample-size limits. (6) Reference list that is complete and consistently formatted. (7) Page count and body length in proportion to the content, with no padding.
what_triggers_block: Figures or tables so illegible or mislabelled that a central result cannot be read; sections missing in a way that stops the reader from evaluating the study (for example no method description); structure so disordered that the argument cannot be followed.
what_triggers_warn: Repetition across sections; inconsistent notation; captions lacking units or run counts; figure text below legible size; tables that overflow columns; a limitations passage that is short or buried; promotional or filler phrasing; reference formatting errors; length that exceeds what the content needs.
