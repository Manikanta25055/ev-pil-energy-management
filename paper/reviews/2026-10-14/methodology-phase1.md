## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory): From the methodology seat, this asks whether the study design can support the claims it makes. For a processor-in-the-loop comparison of learned and rule-based energy management, that means fair baselines (equal tuning effort, same plant, same cycles), enough runs and seeds to separate controller differences from repeat variance, intervals and effect sizes on every headline number, a plant model checked against logged vehicle data, strict train/validation/test separation, honest reporting of any departure from the pre-registered gates, sound latency and memory measurement on the target, and enough released material to repeat the work.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory): I do not rule on literature coverage, which belongs to another reviewer. My part is limited to whether domain quantities and methods are used correctly where they touch the method: drive-cycle definitions, energy accounting conventions, battery and motor model assumptions, and the controller descriptions. A wrong unit, a mis-specified cycle, or an energy metric that cannot be compared across controllers would count here.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory): The conclusion must follow from the evidence as the methods produced it. I will check that the stated verdict (learned versus rule-based) matches the gate results, that explanations of mechanism are backed by logged quantities and not by assertion, that retractions or reversals of earlier findings are stated plainly, and that no claim exceeds what the number of runs and the plant fidelity allow.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high): I read this as whether a reader from machine learning or embedded systems can tell what was measured, how, and under what assumptions. From the methods side, that means the evaluation protocol, metrics and acceptance gates are defined without assuming automotive background, and any claim of wider relevance (to other plants or other microcontrollers) is limited to what the experiments cover.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal): I assess only the parts that bear on method clarity: whether the experimental setup, protocol, tables and figures let a reader follow what was run, with what settings, and how results were aggregated, and whether the structure follows IEEE Access conventions. Prose style is outside my scope.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
dimension_id: D1
what_to_look_for: (1) Baseline fairness: the rule-based controller tuned with effort comparable to the learned one, the same plant, cycles, initial state of charge and noise for every arm; ablations that isolate the learned component. (2) Run counts: number of seeds for training and number of repeat board runs per cell, stated per table. (3) Intervals and effect sizes: confidence or bootstrap intervals, paired differences, repeat-to-repeat spread, and a stated treatment of multiple gates (16 comparisons or similar) with no unreported selection. (4) Plant validation: comparison of the vehicle and battery model against logged data with an error metric, plus a statement of known artefacts (for example, power ceilings that cap billed energy but not motion) and what was done about them. (5) Leakage: separate training, validation and test cycles or scenarios, hyperparameters and gate thresholds fixed before test, no tuning on test cells. (6) Pre-registration: gates and criteria written down before the campaign, with dated deviations, corrections and reversals listed openly. (7) Timing: latency measured on the target with a stated method (cycle counter or GPIO timing), worst case as well as mean, build flags, clock, and memory use, with accuracy reported alongside deploy cost. (8) MEASURED versus SIMULATED labelling on every claim. (9) Energy comparability: a ranking metric that charges shed cabin heat back, not raw consumption. (10) Dropped frames or communication faults counted and excluded by a stated rule. (11) Reproducibility: code, firmware, generated headers, seeds, toolchain versions and data availability, and whether the controller arm (compile-time selected) is verifiable.
what_triggers_block: Any of: a headline claim of a learned-versus-rule difference with a single run, single seed or no repeat variance and no interval; a baseline that is visibly under-tuned or run on a different plant, cycle or noise setting than the learned arm; evidence that gate thresholds, hyperparameters or model selection were tuned on the same cycles used for the reported test result; a plant artefact known to favour one arm that is neither corrected nor bounded, yet the result is presented as a finding; deviations from the pre-registered gates that change the verdict and are not disclosed; on-device claims (latency, memory, accuracy) given with no measurement method or no target-hardware measurement, or SIMULATED numbers presented as MEASURED; no code, firmware or data path of any kind and no description sufficient to reproduce.
what_triggers_warn: Any of: run counts given but intervals or paired effect sizes missing for some headline numbers; no stated correction or accounting for the number of gate comparisons; plant validation against logged data only partial (one cycle, or qualitative); latency reported as a mean without worst case or jitter; some tables missing the run count; board cells with dropped frames kept or removed without a stated threshold; deviations from pre-registration disclosed but scattered or hard to trace; artefact fixes described but the pre-fix results not clearly separated from post-fix results; reproducibility offered only in part (code but not generated headers, seeds or data, or release promised but not yet available); the energy metric defined but not applied uniformly.

### D2: domain_accuracy
dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Correct and standard definitions of drive cycles and their use; energy, regeneration and auxiliary-load accounting that is consistent across controllers; plausible battery, motor and thermal model parameters with units; correct description of the rule-based baseline and of the learned controller's inputs, outputs and constraints; state estimator (Kalman filter) formulation and noise assumptions stated correctly; correct use of processor-in-the-loop terminology as distinct from hardware-in-the-loop; handling of real-time constraints on the microcontroller class used.
what_triggers_block: A domain error that changes a reported result, for example an energy metric that double counts or omits regeneration or auxiliary load for one arm, a cycle or vehicle parameter that makes the comparison physically impossible, or a controller described in a way that contradicts the reported implementation.
what_triggers_warn: Minor unit or notation inconsistencies; a standard cycle used with undocumented modifications; model parameters given without source or validation; terminology (PIL, HIL, SIL) used loosely; estimator noise settings not justified; claims about typical vehicle behaviour without a measured basis.

### D3: argumentative_coherence
dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Whether the abstract, results and conclusion state the same verdict; whether the evidence chain from gates to verdict is complete; whether explanations of why one arm wins or loses are supported by a logged driving quantity; whether earlier positive findings that were later reversed are reported as reversals with the cause; whether the usable result is distinguished from results that do not meet the registered criteria; whether limits (plant fidelity, number of vehicles, sensor model, no road test) are carried through to the strength of the conclusion; absence of survivorship bias (only favourable cells shown) and of post-hoc reframing of failed gates as successes.
what_triggers_block: The headline conclusion contradicts the paper's own tables or gate outcomes; a mechanism claimed as established with no measurement behind it and used to carry the central argument; failed pre-registered criteria recast as passed, or omitted from the abstract and conclusion; results from an artefact-affected setting used as support for the main claim.
what_triggers_warn: Conclusion wording stronger than the interval allows; mechanism explanations offered as likely but untested; limitations stated once but not reflected in the abstract; selective emphasis on favourable cycles or metrics while others are in an appendix; a secondary result (for example the state estimator) presented with more certainty than its evaluation conditions justify.

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether the evaluation protocol, metrics and acceptance gates are explained for readers from machine learning, embedded systems and automotive engineering; whether the processor-in-the-loop setup is described well enough to judge what is real (target silicon, timing) and what is modelled (plant, sensors); whether any generalisation to other vehicles, plants, learning methods or microcontrollers is limited to what was tested; whether the negative or null result is framed as informative for the RL-for-control community with the conditions under which it holds.
what_triggers_block: A broad claim about learned controllers on embedded targets in general that rests on one plant, one microcontroller and one learning method, with no qualification, or a protocol so under-described that an adjacent-field reader cannot tell which parts were measured on hardware.
what_triggers_warn: Domain jargon or acronyms used without definition for non-automotive readers; generalisation sentences not tied to the tested scope; the boundary between host simulation and target measurement stated but easy to miss; implications for deployment given without the on-device cost and accuracy side by side.

### D5: writing_and_structure
dimension_id: D5
what_to_look_for: A methods section that follows the order of the experiment (platform, plant, controllers, protocol, metrics, gates, statistics); tables that carry run counts, units and intervals; figures with readable axes and fonts at IEEE Access column width; consistent naming of controller arms and gates across text, tables and figures; the pre-registration and its deviations in one findable place; IEEE Access conventions for abstract length, reference format, and data availability statement; length proportionate to content at about 11,900 words.
what_triggers_block: Figures or tables that cannot be read or that conflict with the text on key numbers, or a structure in which the protocol and gates cannot be reconstructed from the manuscript.
what_triggers_warn: Inconsistent controller or gate labels; tables without units or counts; figure text too small or colour-only encoding; the deviation list buried in prose; redundancy between sections; missing data availability statement; minor departures from IEEE Access formatting. Placeholder author names, affiliation and dates are not defects, per the contract.
