## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory). From my side as a domain reviewer, this asks whether the experimental design is the kind the EMS and eco-driving community would trust: drive cycles that are recognised (WLTP, US06, UDDS or similar), a longitudinal vehicle model that is physically credible, baselines that are fair, and results that carry run counts and intervals. It also asks whether the evidence is clearly split between what was measured on the target microcontroller and what was only simulated on a host, and whether someone else could rerun it. I will judge only the domain-validity side of this (is the plant and baseline appropriate for the claim); detailed statistics belong to Reviewer 1.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory). This is my core dimension. Claims must agree with current EMS, eco-driving and vehicle-state-estimation evidence. Prior work must be described correctly and the key references must be present. Terminology must be used as the field uses it, including what an "energy manager" actually decides in a battery-electric vehicle with one energy source. There must be no factual errors in vehicle dynamics, regeneration, motor and battery behaviour, or in how drive-cycle results are reported.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory). The central thesis, learned versus rule-based control judged against pre-registered acceptance gates, must hold together. The conclusions must follow from the reported evidence, and any reversal or correction of an earlier finding must be handled honestly. I will check, from a domain view, that comparisons are on equal terms (same energy accounting, same plant, same speed information) and that no step in the argument depends on a simulator artefact.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high). The paper sits between EV energy management, reinforcement learning, state estimation and embedded ML. This asks whether a reader from an adjacent field (control, embedded systems, ML) can follow the definitions, and whether claims that cross into those fields are backed up. For me, it also asks whether the lessons the paper draws about learned control on constrained hardware are stated in a way that transfers to other EMS work.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal). Organisation, clarity, quality of figures and tables, and fit with IEEE Access conventions. From a domain angle I will check that units, symbols and metric definitions (energy per distance, state of charge change, regeneration share) are consistent and that tables can be read without the text.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
dimension_id: D1
what_to_look_for: Use of standard cycles (WLTP, US06, UDDS, or a justified subset) and not a single hand-made profile; a longitudinal model with stated mass, drag, rolling resistance, drivetrain efficiency, regeneration limits and battery limits; whether the plant is the same for all controllers; whether baselines include a well-tuned rule-based or ECMS-style strategy and not a strawman; energy compared on a common basis (for example net battery energy corrected for state-of-charge change and for any cabin or auxiliary load shed); labels separating MEASURED on the microcontroller from SIMULATED on the host; run counts, seeds, intervals; on-device cost (latency, memory, flash) reported next to any accuracy or energy figure; gates fixed before the data were seen, with a record of the order.
what_triggers_block: The headline learned-versus-rule comparison rests on a plant or accounting choice that favours one arm (for example power limits that cap billed energy but not motion, or unequal handling of shed auxiliary load), so the reported difference may be an artefact; or the baseline is clearly untuned; or the main numbers have no run counts and no intervals; or simulated results are presented as measured on the target.
what_triggers_warn: Only one or two cycles, or cycles not named in the standard set; a single seed or small repeat count with intervals missing on secondary claims; vehicle parameters given incompletely; on-device cost missing for some arms; a gate redefined after seeing results without a clear note; an artefact is found and fixed but the earlier affected numbers are not marked as superseded.

### D2: domain_accuracy
dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Correct description of the control problem. In a single-source battery EV there is no power-split between sources, so I will check what the "energy manager" really controls (torque or speed command shaping, regeneration blending, auxiliary and thermal load shedding, speed planning) and whether the paper says so plainly. Literature coverage: classic EMS (rule-based, equivalent consumption minimisation strategy by Paganelli et al., dynamic programming as the optimal benchmark, Pontryagin-based methods, MPC for energy-efficient driving), learning-based EMS (DQN, DDPG, SAC and related deep RL for hybrid and electric powertrains, for example Liu and co-authors, Wu et al., Lian et al., Tang et al., and the surveys on RL-based EMS), eco-driving and speed planning (including connected and automated eco-driving, GLOSA, MPC-based eco-cruise), regenerative braking blending, safe or constrained RL, and embedded deployment of learned controllers (quantisation, TinyML, PIL and HIL practice). For state estimation: Kalman-filter speed or state estimation and sensor-fault detection or fusion in vehicles, with correct use of the term "fault detection and isolation". Also check physical statements: road-load equation, regeneration efficiency, motor and inverter efficiency maps, WLTP and US06 characteristics (US06 is aggressive with high speed and acceleration, UDDS is a low-speed urban cycle), and that energy per distance is not compared across cycles as if it were cycle-independent.
what_triggers_block: A factual error in vehicle or powertrain physics or in cycle properties that changes a conclusion; mischaracterising what a controller class can do in a BEV (for example claiming a power-split optimum in a single-source drivetrain) in a way the claims depend on; a missing body of directly competing work such that the stated novelty is already published (for example prior learned-EMS-on-embedded-target or PIL studies that the paper claims are first); or prior work described wrongly enough that the research gap does not exist.
what_triggers_warn: Three or more key EMS or eco-driving references missing (no ECMS or DP benchmark, no deep-RL EMS survey, no MPC eco-driving work) while the gap argument still stands; secondary citations used where the original is easy to cite; loose terminology (calling a speed or torque shaper an "energy management strategy" without saying so, mixing "efficiency" and "consumption", or "fault detection" with "fault diagnosis"); a bibliography concentrated on hybrid-vehicle EMS with no discussion of how it transfers to BEVs; claims about generalisation to other vehicles or cycles beyond what was tested.

### D3: argumentative_coherence
dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Whether the thesis ("a learned controller is or is not better than rules under pre-registered gates") is stated once and followed through abstract, results and conclusion; whether each gate is defined with a threshold before results and the pass or fail is reported against that exact threshold; whether the abstract and conclusion match the tables, including sign and size of reported gains (a small percentage gain with wide spread must not be called a win); whether negative or reversed results are reported as such; whether explanations of mechanism (why one arm saves energy) are backed by a logged quantity and not asserted; whether the stated contribution matches what the experiments can support; whether the decision on the usable component (for example an estimator) follows from its measured result.
what_triggers_block: The central conclusion contradicts the paper's own tables or gates; a gate is passed in the text but failed in the data (or the reverse); the main claim depends on an effect the paper itself identifies as a plant or accounting artefact without removing it; or circular reasoning where the acceptance criteria were tuned to the result.
what_triggers_warn: Abstract or conclusion wording stronger than the evidence (for example "outperforms" with an interval that overlaps zero); a mechanism explanation not tied to a measurement; ambiguity between exploratory and pre-registered analyses; inconsistencies in numbers between sections; limitations stated but not carried into the conclusions.

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether RL, state-estimation and embedded terms are defined for a reader from vehicle engineering and the reverse; whether the pre-registration idea is explained in a way an engineering reader can use; whether results on the microcontroller (latency, memory, quantisation effects, real-time deadlines, dropped frames) are reported in terms an embedded-ML reader trusts; whether the implications for deploying learned controllers in production vehicles (functional safety, ISO 26262-style concerns, validation burden, comparison to calibrated rule sets) are discussed and not just implied.
what_triggers_block: Claims about embedded or learning behaviour that are plainly unsupported by any on-device measurement, or interdisciplinary claims (for example about safety or certification) that are false or invented.
what_triggers_warn: Undefined jargon crossing the RL and vehicle fields; no discussion of safety, certification or calibration practice when proposing a learned controller for a vehicle; implications for other EMS settings (hybrids, fuel cells, other vehicle classes) asserted without support; the pre-registration method presented as new without reference to existing practice in ML or engineering evaluation.

### D5: writing_and_structure
dimension_id: D5
what_to_look_for: IEEE Access structure (abstract, introduction with a clear gap and contribution list, related work, method, experiments, results, discussion, limitations, conclusion); consistent symbols and units; tables that state cycle, arm, run count and metric; figures with readable axes and units; a clear flag on every number as measured or simulated; related work that synthesises and does not merely list; length proportionate (about 11,900 words in 18 pages is dense but acceptable for the venue).
what_triggers_block: Not applicable as a normal outcome for this normal-priority dimension; use block only if the manuscript is so disorganised or its tables and figures so unreadable that the domain claims cannot be checked.
what_triggers_warn: Inconsistent notation or units between text and tables; missing run counts or interval notation in table captions; related work as a list; figures whose text is too small or whose axes lack units; a contribution list that overstates what the results section delivers; repeated content across sections.
